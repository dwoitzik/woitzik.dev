## Why This Comparison Exists at All

The Pi 5 is a good board. People search for "alternatives" because of availability and price, not a spec deficiency. Raspberry Pi's retail pricing has crept up since the Pi 4 era, and RK3588 boards from Radxa, Orange Pi, and others outperform it on paper for similar money. Whether that advantage survives real homelab use is the question.

## Selection Criteria for Homelab Edge Use

**Software and driver maturity.** This is where the Pi wins before specs enter the conversation. Raspberry Pi OS, a kernel Raspberry Pi maintains itself, and a decade of community documentation mean almost everything works, including obscure GPIO/HAT hardware. RK3588 boards run mainline Linux reasonably well in 2026, but VPU/NPU acceleration, some peripheral drivers, and firmware still lag behind. Vendor kernel forks and less-tested distro images are common.

**Actual (not marketed) idle power draw.** For anything running 24/7 on a homelab shelf, idle wattage matters more than peak benchmark numbers.

**Long-term software support.** Raspberry Pi states the Pi 5 will stay in production until at least January 2036. Smaller board vendors support their boards too, but for shorter and less predictable periods. That matters if you don't want to re-flash and re-provision in two years.

**Networking.** The Pi 5 has a single 1GbE port. Both RK3588 boards here ship 2.5GbE, which matters if the device does anything network-intensive (routing, NAS-adjacent duties).

**Price at the RAM tier you need.** SBC pricing is not linear across RAM tiers, and the advertised starting price of any board is the smallest variant, not the 16GB one you'd want for anything beyond DNS.

## The Boards

### Raspberry Pi 5 (8GB): Best Overall

Broadcom BCM2712, 4x Cortex-A76 @ 2.4GHz, LPDDR4X in 1/2/4/8/16GB variants, single 1GbE, and a PCIe 2.0 x1 interface that takes an M.2 HAT for NVMe boot. The [db-tronic starter kit I've linked throughout this site*](/go/4wETNfv) bundles the 8GB board with a case, fan, official power supply, and SD card, which saves a separate shopping list if you don't already have those parts lying around.

The Pi 5's real advantage over its predecessor and most alternatives isn't raw compute. It's the PCIe lane and the official HAT ecosystem around it, which finally make NVMe boot a well-documented, first-class option instead of a community hack. For homelab edge roles (DNS, Keepalived, Home Assistant, lightweight k3s agents) the Pi 5 has enough CPU headroom, and the software maturity means you spend your time on the service instead of debugging why a USB Wi-Fi dongle or camera module doesn't work on one particular board revision.

**Pros:** unmatched software/community support, official case and HAT ecosystem, production commitment to 2036, PoE+ via HAT
**Cons:** single 1GbE only, NVMe needs a separate HAT, price has crept up since the Pi 4 era

### Radxa Rock 5B: Best Raw Performance

Rockchip RK3588 (4x Cortex-A76 + 4x Cortex-A55), 4/8/16GB LPDDR4X, one 2.5GbE port, and an on-board M.2 M-key slot (PCIe 3.0 x4) for NVMe, so no HAT needed.

On paper this is a much more powerful board than the Pi 5. Eight cores, the RK3588's hardware video codecs, and its 6 TOPS NPU give it an edge for anything that can use that hardware (video transcoding, some ML inference), and the on-board M.2 slot is a real convenience. The tradeoff is software maturity: mainline kernel support has improved a lot, but you're more likely to hit a driver gap or need a vendor image for full hardware support. NPU acceleration in particular is still the least mature part of the RK3588 software stack across every board, not just this one.

**Pros:** more compute headroom than the Pi 5, on-board NVMe slot with four PCIe 3.0 lanes, 2.5GbE
**Cons:** smaller community than Pi, less mature driver/firmware stack, shorter and less predictable support window, fewer case/accessory options

### Orange Pi 5 Plus: Best Specs-per-Euro

Same RK3588 SoC as the Rock 5B, 4/8/16/32GB RAM, dual 2.5GbE, on-board M.2 M-key slot (PCIe 3.0 x4).

Orange Pi has historically undercut Radxa on price for the same silicon, and the 32GB configuration is unusual in this class. It's useful if the plan is running several lightweight VMs via KVM rather than bare containers. Orange Pi's documentation and image quality have historically been rougher than Radxa's, which are in turn rougher than Raspberry Pi's. If you hit a hardware quirk, expect more time in forum threads and less in polished documentation.

**Pros:** most RAM headroom in this comparison, dual 2.5GbE, on-board NVMe
**Cons:** documentation and image quality behind Radxa and Pi, smallest community of the three, needs an active cooler under sustained load

## Who Should Buy Which

**Buy the Raspberry Pi 5 if:** you want the widest hardware/software compatibility, you're running GPIO or camera peripherals, or the workload is light (DNS, Keepalived, Home Assistant) and driver headaches aren't worth risking for compute you don't need.

**Buy the Rock 5B or Orange Pi 5 Plus if:** the workload is CPU- or RAM-bound (multiple KVM VMs, video transcoding, light ML inference) and you're comfortable working through a smaller community's documentation when something doesn't work out of the box.

**Buy an N100 mini PC instead if:** the project doesn't need GPIO or the smallest physical footprint. For pure homelab service hosting, x86 driver maturity beats every SBC option here.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is the Rock 5B actually faster than the Raspberry Pi 5?

In multi-threaded benchmarks and anything that can use the RK3588's NPU or video codecs, yes, by a clear margin. In homelab service hosting (reverse proxies, DNS, lightweight containers) the difference is far less noticeable than the spec sheet suggests, because those workloads rarely saturate either board's CPU.

### Should I buy an RK3588 board for k3s?

For a worker node running a handful of lightweight pods, either family works. The Pi 5 now goes up to 16GB, which covers most edge-node use. If you need more than that, the 32GB Orange Pi 5 Plus is the only option here. Budget extra time for kernel/driver troubleshooting on either RK3588 board if you hit an edge case.

### Does the Raspberry Pi 5 support NVMe boot?

Yes, via the official M.2 HAT+ (or third-party equivalents) on the PCIe 2.0 x1 interface. It's a separate purchase, unlike the Rock 5B and Orange Pi 5 Plus, which have the M.2 slot on-board.

### What about power draw?

All three boards idle in the low single-digit watts in published measurements. The differences are a few watts at most, not a category difference, and not enough on their own to decide anything for a homelab running a handful of these devices.

## Conclusion

The Raspberry Pi 5 is no longer the best SBC on paper. The RK3588 boards from Radxa and Orange Pi out-spec it for similar money. What the Pi still wins decisively is the thing that costs you time in a homelab: software and driver maturity. For DNS, Keepalived, and similar edge roles, I'm staying on the Raspberry Pi platform, and the Pi 5 is what I'd buy to replace my 4Bs. For CPU- or RAM-bound work where you'll trade some driver risk for more silicon, the Rock 5B or Orange Pi 5 Plus is worth it. And for pure service hosting with no GPIO requirement, a budget x86 mini PC often beats every SBC in this comparison on total reliability per euro.
