## The Order That Matters

Buy in this order, not cheapest first. Compute without a way to network it is useless, and a NAS without a backup strategy is a single point of failure with extra steps.

1. **Compute** (a mini PC or a proper server, depending on scale)
2. **Network** (the router/firewall; retrofitting VLANs onto a flat network later is real work)
3. **Backup target** (before you have data worth losing, not after)
4. **Storage/NAS** (once compute and network are settled)
5. **Rack, cooling, and tools** (quality-of-life, not blocking)

## Tier 1: Starter (~€450-600)

The goal at this tier is one always-on box that can run Proxmox or a bare Docker host, plus a router capable of at least basic VLANs.

**Compute:** an Intel N100 mini PC. I've compared the current options in [Best Mini PC for Homelab 2026](/blog/best-mini-pc-homelab-2026/). The short version is the Beelink S12 Pro (around €350, a single 2.5GbE port, 7-9W idle draw). The dual-NIC EQ12 this guide used to recommend is no longer sold on amazon.de.

**Network:** at this budget, a consumer router with VLAN support is defensible, but if IaC-managed networking is part of the point of the homelab, the [MikroTik RB5009*](/go/4w08b21) at ~€180 is worth stretching for even at this tier. The [MikroTik vs Ubiquiti comparison](/blog/mikrotik-vs-ubiquiti-home-network-2026/) explains why.

**Backup:** a directly attached external drive is the minimum viable backup target. I use a [Seagate 2TB Portable HDD*](/go/4vxwLpY) for exactly this. It is not a 3-2-1 strategy on its own, but have it from day one, whatever you add later.

## Tier 2: Serious Homelab (~€800-1200)

This is where a homelab stops being "a box under the desk" and starts needing rack infrastructure, redundant edge devices, and a proper NAS.

**Compute:** step up to a Ryzen mobile chip if you're running more than a handful of containers or any local LLM inference. I run an older BMAX B5A Pro; the current BMAX Ryzen 7 8745HS is its successor. The [hardware tour](/blog/homelab-hardware-tour-physical-rack/) documents what is in my rack.

**Storage/NAS:** a 2-bay NAS with real RAID 1 redundancy. My pick and the reasoning behind it are in [Best NAS for Home Backup 2026](/blog/best-nas-home-backup-2026/); currently that is the Synology DS225+.

**Edge/DNS redundancy:** a pair of Raspberry Pis for DNS with Keepalived failover, so DNS is not a single point of failure. I still run the two Pi 4Bs I started with; if you are buying today, the [Raspberry Pi 5 Alternatives Compared](/blog/raspberry-pi-5-alternatives-2026/) covers the current options and the performance difference.

**Rack basics:** a compact enclosure keeps cable chaos contained once you're past two or three devices. I use a [DIGITUS 10-Inch 6U Wall Mounting Cabinet*](/go/4pGBkxb); the hardware tour explains 10" vs 19".

## Tier 3: Full Rack (~€1500+)

At this point you're running a full Proxmox + k3s stack with proper cooling, power monitoring, and cable management, as documented in the hardware tour.

**Cooling:** a closed rack traps heat faster than it looks like it should. I added [ARCTIC P12 120mm Fans*](/go/44vgXJy) as exhaust after watching NVMe temperatures hit 72°C under sustained summer load. The full story is in the hardware tour.

**Power:** a rack PDU ([DIGITUS 4-Way 10-Inch PDU*](/go/3RiBttM)) and a power-monitoring smart plug ([TP-Link Tapo P110*](/go/3STKPwD)). The plug matters more than it sounds, especially at German electricity prices: knowing your real idle and load wattage tells you whether the next upgrade is worth the running cost.

**Tools:** an [iFixit Pro Tech Toolkit*](/go/4wKbVEW) for opening mini PCs and swapping M.2 drives, a [Brother PT-H110 Label Maker*](/go/4wcr4PG) (buy this before you have a rack, not after the third time you trace an unlabeled cable), and reusable [Velcro cable ties*](/go/4bnvY3K) instead of zip ties you'll cut off and rebuy.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need to buy everything in Tier 3 to have a "real" homelab?

No. Tier 1 is a useful homelab: one box, basic backup, a network you understand. Tiers 2 and 3 solve problems (redundancy, thermal headroom, cable sanity) you'll only hit once you're running enough services to notice them.

### Is the MikroTik RB5009 overkill for a Tier 1 budget?

For pure routing, yes; a cheaper router works. But retrofitting VLANs and Terraform-managed firewall rules onto a network you built without them later is real, avoidable rework. If infrastructure-as-code is part of why you're building a homelab at all, front-loading the router purchase is the one Tier 1 recommendation worth stretching the budget for.

### What's the single highest-value upgrade from Tier 1 to Tier 2?

The second Raspberry Pi for DNS redundancy. A single DNS resolver going down takes the whole network's name resolution with it, and active/passive Keepalived failover is cheap compared to almost anything else on this list.

### Should I buy new or used mini PCs / NAS units?

Used mini PCs are generally fine if you can verify hours of use and check the thermal paste; used NAS units are riskier because you're inheriting unknown drive history alongside the enclosure. For the NAS specifically, I'd buy new.

## Conclusion

Buy compute and network first, backup before you have data worth losing, storage once the foundation is settled, and treat rack, cooling, and tools as the layer that turns "it works" into "it works reliably and I can maintain it."
